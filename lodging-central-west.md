# Lodging — Blue Mountains, Bathurst, Orange, Mudgee

Research note, 30 Aug 2026. This is the corridor between Sydney and Dubbo — the natural
"drive west from Sydney" zone, all of it inside the path. It has far more lodging than
[Bourke](lodging-bourke.md), but it also has the most competition for it, and a weather
subtlety that cuts against the most obvious choice.

## Headline: this region is already being planned for as a mass event

The Central West councils and tourism bodies are **actively preparing** for July 2028:

- Bathurst Regional Council's acting director for Cultural and Community Services: *"It is almost
  certain that Bathurst will be 100 per cent full for the period of the eclipse."*
- Ray Pickard (Bathurst Observatory) estimates eclipse events draw ~200,000 to prime viewing
  locations, adding *"even if we halve that estimate, that's what the region could expect"* —
  so call it 100,000+ against a Bathurst LGA population of **43,567**.
- Council has **already** opened discussions with accommodation providers, and Pickard reported
  **booking inquiries from the United States as of 2026** — two years out.
- It is the region's first total eclipse since **1857**, and the next is **2858**. Local media are
  running that line, which tells you how it will be marketed.

Read that as: **this corridor books out early and hard.** Unlike Sydney, it cannot absorb demand.

## The weather catch — the Blue Mountains are not the answer

This is the part that changes the recommendation.

[Eclipsophile](https://eclipsophile.com/tse2028/) is explicit that the mountains cut both ways:

> "The Blue Mountains are not particularly high as mountains go, but for the eclipse seeker, they
> have an important influence on weather prospects, **both good and bad**."

**The bad:** *"Any passing weather disturbance will have heavier, thicker, and longer-lasting cloud
cover and heavier precipitation as it encounters the mountains."* Orographic lift means the
elevated ground manufactures its own cloud. The Blue Mountains tourist industry markets winter mist
and fog as an attraction — sitting by the fire watching cloud fill the Jamison Valley is the
product. That is precisely the wrong thing to be standing in at 2 p.m. on eclipse day.

**The good, and where it points:** *"a drop of about 8 percent in average cloudiness on the
centreline as the elevation falls, reflecting the tendency of sinking air to dry out as it descends
mountain slopes."*

So the favourable zone is the **descent east of the range** — the lower mountains and the western
Sydney basin — not the tops. That points at **Penrith**, which has **3 min 48 s of totality**
(identical to Sydney city), sits on the drying side of the slope, and is ordinary Greater Sydney
with unlimited lodging and a rail line.

⚠️ **Verify before relying on this.** The 8% figure is sourced; "therefore Penrith" is my inference
from it. Check it against a centreline cloud map before it drives a booking.

Corroborating signal, independent of the eclipse: **Rex suspends its morning and evening Orange
flights from 21 June to 15 August** each winter for "challenging" winter weather — a window that
contains eclipse day. Midday services continue, and the eclipse is at ~2 p.m., which is the good
part of the day. But an airline restructuring its schedule around Central Tablelands winter fog is
a data point about the region, not a footnote.

## Regional cloud, for comparison

| Location | Mean July cloud | Totality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mudgee | just under 40% | TBD — verify |
| Bathurst / Orange (tablelands) | ~40%, higher off-centreline to the south | ~3 min 47 s (Orange) |
| Blue Mountains / Katoomba | elevated — worse in any disturbance | 3 min 47 s |
| **Lower mountains / western basin** | **~8% below the ridge tops** | ~3 min 48 s |
| Sydney (coastal) | ~40–47% | 3 min 48 s |

Eclipsophile's warning about the coast applies to this whole eastern end: *"Sydney's weather in
2028 could be much like Cairns in 2012, where some watchers saw the eclipse while others, only a
short distance away, were disappointed by an unfortunate cloud."* Mobility on the day matters more
here than the choice of town.

## The lodging itself

### Blue Mountains / Katoomba — most stock, most atmosphere, worst odds

Katoomba (pop. 8,268) anchors a mature tourist region: five-star hotels, historic guesthouses,
boutique cottages, self-contained cabins, and large-group properties (one operator accommodates up
to ~700 across lodges and dorms). Self-contained cottages sleeping 10–14 exist, so **a whole house
for six is easy to find here** — easier than anywhere else on this list.

- **For:** the widest range of whole-house options, genuinely lovely, 1.5–2 h from Sydney by car or
  train, and a real destination for the surrounding days.
- **Against:** the orographic cloud problem above; **average winter temperature around 5°C** in the
  Upper Mountains with morning frost; and the Great Western Highway is the single spine through the
  region — eclipse-day congestion on it is a near-certainty, especially as it is also the escape
  route for anyone driving west out of Sydney chasing clear sky.

### Bathurst — the region's focal point, and therefore the most contested

Pop. 43,567 (LGA). Real hotel stock: **Rydges Mount Panorama (~131 rooms)**, Quality Hotel Bathurst
(~50), Bathurst Explorers Motel (~40), Country Lodge Motor Inn (~32), plus substantial motel and
pub accommodation. A city used to hosting large motorsport crowds, so it has event experience.

That experience is also the problem: Bathurst is positioning itself as *the* regional eclipse
destination, with the observatory, a planned weekend programme, and possible NASA broadcast
involvement. **Expect it to be the first place in the corridor to sell out**, and expect
event pricing.

### Orange — the best-balanced regional option

Pop. 43,512 (LGA). A cool-climate wine and food region with good quality accommodation, and
**Orange Regional Airport** served by QantasLink, Rex and Link Airways — around 25 return flights a
week to Sydney. Totality **3 min 47 s at 1:57 p.m.**

For a group of six who want a comfortable regional base with proper restaurants and the option to
fly rather than drive from Sydney, Orange is the strongest of the tablelands towns — with the
winter-schedule caveat above.

### Mudgee — quieter, wine country, slightly better cloud

Just under 40% cloud, the best of the tablelands figures. Smaller than Bathurst or Orange with a
well-developed wine-tourism accommodation base — cottages and guesthouses that suit a group of six.
Less likely to be the focus of official eclipse events, which may make it easier to book.

## What to do now

1. **Do not book the Blue Mountains for eclipse night on the assumption that higher and further west
   is better.** For this eclipse it is plausibly worse than staying in the Sydney basin.
2. **If the group wants a Central West base for the trip's character** — wine country, Bathurst
   observatory, regional food — book Orange or Mudgee, and treat eclipse morning as a drive to
   wherever the forecast is best. Everything from Bathurst to the coast is in the path, so you can
   move a long way without leaving totality.
3. **Book early if at all.** Bathurst council is on record expecting 100% occupancy, and US
   inquiries were already arriving in 2026. This is not Bourke's problem of tiny inventory; it is
   the opposite problem of enormous demand against moderate inventory.
4. **Price a whole house.** For six people across three couples, the Blue Mountains and Mudgee both
   have good self-contained stock, which beats three motel rooms for a week.

## The honest recommendation

**This corridor is a better place to spend the trip than to spend eclipse day.**

The tablelands' cloud advantage over Sydney is small (~40% vs ~40–47%), the Blue Mountains'
elevation is an active liability, and the whole region will be far more contested for beds than
Sydney will. Meanwhile Penrith and the western Sydney basin appear to offer the corridor's best
cloud odds with none of the lodging difficulty and effectively identical totality.

The strongest use of this region: **stay in Sydney or the western basin for eclipse
night, and use the Blue Mountains, Bathurst, Orange and Mudgee for the days around it** — where the
cloud does not matter and the region is at its best.

That is consistent with, and strengthens, the argument in [`site-selection.md`](site-selection.md):
the NSW option's real advantage is a several-hundred-kilometre escape route inside the path, and
the way to use it is to stay mobile, not to commit to elevated ground.
