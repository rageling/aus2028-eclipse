# The total solar eclipse of 22 July 2028

On **Saturday, 22 July 2028**, the Moon's shadow crosses Australia from the Kimberley coast in
Western Australia to the Tasman Sea, passing over the Northern Territory north of Alice Springs,
clipping southwest Queensland, and running southeast across New South Wales **directly over Sydney
Harbour** before ending at sunset over New Zealand's South Island.

Greatest duration is **5 minutes 10 seconds** — among the dozen longest total eclipses of the 21st
century. It is the first total eclipse over Sydney since 1857. The next is in 2858.

These are the working research notes for one group's attempt to decide where to stand. They are
published because the underlying research — cloud climatology, totality durations, and what lodging
actually exists in the small towns along the path — was tedious to assemble and may save someone
else the same work.

## Three things that are easy to get wrong

**Broome, Halls Creek, Alice Springs and Newcastle are not in the path.** All four are the towns
people naturally reach for, and all four miss it. Verify path membership per town against a
centreline map; never infer it from proximity.

**Altitude does not buy you clearer sky here.** The Blue Mountains manufacture their own cloud in any
disturbance. Cloudiness *falls* by roughly 8% as you descend the western slopes — which points at the
Sydney basin floor, not the ridge above it.

**The inland squeeze has already started.** The Devils Marbles Hotel was fully booked for eclipse
night as of 2026. Small-town inventory along the path is measured in dozens of beds, not hundreds.

## Please verify anything you rely on

Every factual claim on these pages carries a source link, and anything unverified is marked
`TBD — verify`. But these are one group's working notes, not a reference work — figures were correct
when checked and may have drifted since, and some are inferences drawn from a source rather than
statements made by it. Where it matters, follow the link and read the source.

**This applies most of all to eye safety.** Never look at the partial phases without an ISO 12312-2
filter, and never look through a camera, binoculars or a telescope with eclipse glasses as your only
protection. See [gear and safety](gear-and-safety.md), then check it against
[the AAS reputable-vendors list](https://eclipse.aas.org/eye-safety/viewers-filters) yourself.
