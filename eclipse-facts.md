# The eclipse

Every number on this page carries a source. Nothing here is from memory — several
"obvious" assumptions turned out to be wrong (see [What is *not* in the path](#what-is-not-in-the-path)).

## The essentials

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Date** | **Saturday, 22 July 2028** — a weekend, which helps for group travel |
| **Greatest duration** | 5 min 10 s — among the dozen longest total eclipses of the 21st century |
| **Point of greatest eclipse** | 15°36′S 126°42′E, Kimberley coast, Western Australia — 02:56:40 UTC |
| **Saros / magnitude** | Saros 146 (member 28 of 76) / magnitude 1.056 |

The path enters at the **Kimberley coast (WA)**, crosses the **Northern Territory** north of Alice
Springs, clips **southwest Queensland**, then runs southeast across **New South Wales and directly
over Sydney Harbour** before crossing the Tasman and ending at sunset over New Zealand's South Island.

Totality happens around **11:00 AWST** in the Kimberley, **~12:45 ACST** in the Northern Territory,
and **~14:00 AEST** in Sydney.

## What is *not* in the path

Worth stating explicitly, because each of these is a natural guess and each is **wrong**:

- **Broome (WA)** — partial only.
- **Halls Creek (WA)** — not in the path, despite having the best sunshine record on the track (>93% of daylight hours).
- **Alice Springs (NT)** — partial only. The track passes well *north* of it.
- **Newcastle (NSW)** — outside the path. The northern edge of the Greater Sydney totality zone is
  Lake Macquarie, just short of Newcastle. This is a near-edge call — **re-verify against a
  centreline map before anyone books Newcastle.**

## Cloud climatology — the whole reason site selection is hard

Mean July cloud cover along the track:

| Segment | Cloud | Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kimberley coast → Karlu Karlu | **<10%** | Halls Creek records sunshine >93% of daylight hours |
| NT / Stuart Highway | ~15–20% | Sunshine frequency above 90% |
| SW Queensland (Bedourie–Thargomindah) | ~25% | Satellite readings may be inflated by bright salt lakes |
| Inland NSW → Blue Mountains | just under 40% at Mudgee | Rises as the track nears the coast |
| **Sydney** | **~40–47%** | Satellite 2000–2025: 9 clear days, 12 partly clearing, 5 fully overcast |

So the trade runs in a straight line: **the clearer the sky, the harder the logistics.** That
tension is resolved in [`site-selection.md`](site-selection.md), which is the decision of record.

### Weather hazards on the inland track

Not just cloud. July is the dry season, but the record includes:

- Extreme rainfall events (Bedourie's record is 137 mm; 50 mm+ events are possible)
- Winds above 70 km/h from passing low-pressure systems
- Dust storms accompanying those winds
- **Gaps in mobile coverage** — relevant to any plan that depends on checking a forecast on the morning of

July is Australian winter: cool nights inland (genuinely cold before dawn in the desert), mild days
in Sydney.
