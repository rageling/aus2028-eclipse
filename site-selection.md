# Site selection — the decision of record

**Status: OPEN.** Due **early 2027**. Everything else in this repo waits on it.

This is the only genuinely hard decision on the trip. The date is fixed, the group is fixed, and
Australia is a long way away regardless. What varies — enormously — is where the group is
standing at totality, and that choice trades **the odds of actually seeing it** against **how hard
the trip is on the four older travellers in it**.

## The trade, stated plainly

Cloud probability in July falls almost monotonically as you move inland and northwest, from ~47% at
Sydney to under 10% on the Kimberley coast. Logistical difficulty rises along exactly the same axis.
There is no site that is both easy and clear. See [`eclipse-facts.md`](eclipse-facts.md) for the
sourced climatology.

## Candidates

A full inventory of **every place in the path that has lodging** — including the Channel Country and
Kimberley towns this table only samples — is in [`options.md`](options.md).

Scored for a specific brief: **six people, four of them older travellers.**
`Access` means getting there from an international arrival; `Bailout` means the ability to move on
eclipse morning if the forecast turns.

**Revision, 30 Aug 2026 — the Blue Mountains moved down and Penrith moved up.** Elevation is a
liability for this eclipse, not an asset: the mountains manufacture their own cloud in any passing
disturbance, while sinking air on the eastern descent dries out, giving roughly **8% lower average
cloudiness as elevation falls along the centreline**. That points to the lower mountains and western
basin. Detail and caveats in [`lodging-central-west.md`](lodging-central-west.md).

## The structural advantage of the NSW option

This deserves its own heading because it is easy to miss and it changes the calculus.

**The entire Greater Sydney basin is inside the path, from Lake Macquarie to Kiama** — and the path
continues unbroken west through Penrith, Katoomba, Bathurst, Orange, Dubbo and on to Bourke. That is
several hundred kilometres of sealed highway, all of it in totality.

So a Sydney-based group is not making a single 47% bet. On eclipse morning you can read the
forecast and drive west along the path, staying inside totality the whole way, putting real distance
between yourselves and coastal cloud. **The bad-weather hedge does not require leaving the path.**

No inland site offers this. At Kununurra or Tennant Creek, if it clouds over you are hundreds of
kilometres from an alternative on roads with patchy mobile coverage — and you will have spent far
more to be there.

That reframes the comparison: it is not 47% versus 10%. It is *47%-with-a-several-hundred-kilometre
escape route* versus *10%-with-none*, at very different costs in money and physical difficulty.

## What would push the decision inland anyway

Honest counterweight — the inland case is not weak:

- **5m10s versus 3m48s** is not a rounding difference. It is 36% more totality, and totality is the
  entire point.
- Under 10% cloud is close to a guarantee. Forty-seven percent is a coin flip on a once-in-a-lifetime
  event that six people flew to the other side of the world for.
- The Kimberley and the Red Centre are extraordinary destinations in their own right. If the trip is
  going to be long anyway, the eclipse can be the anchor rather than the whole of it.

## Open questions before the decision

1. **Mobility and stamina — the real constraint.** How much driving, heat, dust and distance-from-a-hospital
   is reasonable for the four older travellers? This is the least-known input, and it
   probably decides the whole question. Ask directly rather than assuming.
2. **Trip length.** A Sydney-anchored trip works at 10 days. A Kimberley trip does not really work
   under two weeks once domestic flights are counted.
3. **Who drives?** An inland plan means long shifts on remote highways.
4. **Budget ceiling per household.** Kununurra roughly doubles the
   domestic-travel cost of a Sydney trip.
5. **Verify the remaining durations** in `eclipse-facts.md` before ranking on numbers still marked TBD.

## Why the deadline is early 2027

Accommodation inventory in the inland towns is tiny — Bourke, Tennant Creek and Kununurra have a
few hundred beds between them against a global eclipse-chasing audience, and eclipse-path lodging
historically sells out 18–24 months ahead. **If the answer is inland, it has to be booked
essentially the moment it is decided.**

Sydney has far more inventory — ~26,000 CBD rooms plus ~8,700 in Western Sydney — but
**it is not unlimited, and an earlier draft of this file said it was.** The 2024 US eclipse pushed
hotel vacancy in path-of-totality cities **below 1%, with price premiums over 100%**. Sydney is the
most forgiving option on this list by a wide margin; it is not a book-it-whenever option. Plan on
booking Sydney by **early-to-mid 2027** as well — just without the same cliff.

So the asymmetry still sets the schedule, in degree rather than in kind: missing early 2027 forecloses
the inland options entirely, and makes the Sydney option merely expensive.

**Deciding late is therefore close to deciding Sydney, at a worse price.** That may well be the right
answer, but it should be chosen, not defaulted into.
