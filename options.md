# Options — every place in totality that has a bed

Master inventory, compiled 30 Aug 2026. The filter is deliberate: **confirmed inside the path of
totality, and has somewhere to sleep.** Places in the path with no lodging are listed at the bottom
so we know they were considered and excluded, not overlooked.

Deep dives: [Dubbo](lodging-dubbo.md) · [Bourke](lodging-bourke.md) · [Central West](lodging-central-west.md).
The decision itself lives in [`site-selection.md`](site-selection.md).

---

## What this sweep changed

Three things worth knowing before the table:

1. **Southwest Queensland's Channel Country beats Bourke on both axes** — 4m21s–4m37s of totality
   against Bourke's 4m06s, and ~25% cloud against ~30%. It was barely in our candidate set. It is
   also the hardest place on this list to actually stay.
2. **The inland squeeze has already started.** The Devils Marbles Hotel near Tennant Creek is
   **already fully booked for eclipse night** — it took its first eclipse inquiry within hours of
   changing management in October 2024 and has blocked out 2028 entirely.
3. **Sydney is not unlimited, and I said it was.** Correcting that: the 2024 US eclipse pushed
   hotel vacancy in path cities **below 1% with price premiums of 100%+**. Sydney CBD has ~26,000
   rooms and Western Sydney ~8,700 — large, but a finite pool against what may be the city's
   busiest tourism weekend in a generation. Sydney remains the *easiest* option by a wide margin;
   it is not a bookable-at-any-time option.

## In the path but effectively no lodging

Betoota (QLD), Eulo and Currawinya (QLD, ~4m18s), and the many unnamed centreline points along the
Stuart Highway and Channel Country roads. Viable only as day-trip destinations from a Tier 1–2 base,
or fully self-sufficient camping.

## Explicitly NOT in the path

Do not book these. Each is a natural guess and each is wrong: **Broome**, **Halls Creek**,
**Alice Springs**, **Newcastle**. Halls Creek is the cruel one — the best sunshine record on the
entire track (>93% of daylight hours) and outside totality.

## The three real shapes of this trip

**A. Sydney basin.** 3m48s, ~40–47% cloud, everything easy. Book by early 2027 given the vacancy
warning. Eclipse morning, drive west along the path away from coastal cloud — several hundred km of
sealed highway, all in totality. **The hedge does not require leaving the path.** Lowest cost,
lowest strain, lowest odds.

**B. Kununurra.** ~5 minutes, under 10% cloud — the best combination available anywhere on Earth
that day, in a town with hotels, an airport and a tourism board actively preparing. The costs are
real: a long domestic leg from any international gateway, remote-town medical, and eclipse-week
pricing. But **this is the only place that is simultaneously near-optimal for viewing and genuinely
equipped to host people.** If the group's appetite allows it, this is the serious alternative to
Sydney — not Bourke, and not the Blue Mountains.

**C. Dubbo as the middle path.** ~3m50s, ~35–40% cloud, real airports, real hospitals, real
hotels, and still inside the path with the same westward escape route. Modest cloud gain over
Sydney for modest added effort. The compromise that gives up the least.

## What I would drop from consideration

- **Blue Mountains for eclipse night** — elevation manufactures cloud; the ~8% drying effect is on
  the descent, not the tops.
- **Bourke** — its edge over Dubbo is 16 seconds, against far worse access and ~150 beds.
- **Channel Country and Karlu Karlu** — the durations are superb and the skies are excellent, but
  self-sufficiency requirements (10–20 L water per person, spare tyres, satellite messenger) rule
  them out for four travellers in their sixties-plus.

## Verify before booking

| Claim | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Channel Country durations (Birdsville 4m37.4s etc.) | From one specialist guide, and quoted as *centreline* targets near each town, not town centres |
| Kununurra "more than five minutes" | Official tourism-board wording; max 5m10s is near the Drysdale River, so Kununurra is likely just under. Get a precise figure |
| Tennant Creek ~3m26s | Single secondary source |
| "Penrith has the best local cloud odds" | The ~8% descent figure is sourced; the conclusion is my inference. Check a centreline cloud map |
| Sydney sub-1% vacancy | Extrapolated from the 2024 US eclipse, not a Sydney forecast |
| Bathurst 200,000 visitors | One observatory owner's estimate, halved by his own hedge |
| Room counts throughout | Published figures conflict between sources; confirm with the property |
